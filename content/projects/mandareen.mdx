Mandareen is an AI-powered language tutor — a SaaS product I built solo, from the database schema to the deploy pipeline. The pitch was simple: generate lessons on demand and hold something closer to a real conversation with a student than a fixed curriculum.

### The shape

The stack is a Next.js frontend talking to a NestJS API, with PostgreSQL and Prisma underneath and pgvector doing the embedding storage. Supabase handles auth and a chunk of the data layer, S3 sits behind the media. Lessons aren’t pre-written — they’re generated per session, and the chat has to feel live, not like a form with a submit button.

### The AI layer

Generation runs on OpenAI, with Gemini producing the embeddings that pgvector searches over. Speech-to-text and text-to-speech turn the exchange into something you can actually talk through instead of type, and semantic search is what lets a lesson pull in the context that’s actually relevant instead of whatever matches literally.

### The performance work

The first version was slow in the way that kills a “conversation” product: every turn round-tripped to the retriever. I put an LRU cache in front of the hot lookups, batched the embedding calls instead of firing them one at a time, ran independent lookups in parallel instead of sequentially, and moved the vector index onto HNSW. Together that cut retrieval query volume by 99.8% and took end-to-end processing from 5.3 seconds to 0.37 — see [[rag-query-volume|cutting RAG query volume by 99.8%]] for the retrieval side of that story.

Deployment is split across Vercel for the frontend, Railway for the API, and Supabase/S3 for data and media — which meant most of the debugging that mattered wasn’t inside any one service, it was in the seams between them.
